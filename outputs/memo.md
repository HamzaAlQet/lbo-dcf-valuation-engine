## 1. Executive Summary & Deal Thesis

We evaluated a hypothetical take-private of Apple Inc. at 8.0x FY2015 EBITDA of $82.5B, an enterprise value of $659.9B and an implied offer of $142.59 per share (24.3% premium). The projection reaches 33.6% EBITDA margins in the final year and $281.5B of cumulative unlevered free cash flow over five years. The base-case return is 7.5% IRR and 1.43x MOIC, against a 20% hurdle.

- **Cash generation and deleveraging:** 67.5% of acquisition debt is repaid by exit, from 4.0x EBITDA at close.
- **Equity cheque:** $540.0B of sponsor equity (61.5% of uses) with debt sized at 50% of EV, a scale no sponsor has written.
- **Offshore cash:** $186.9B is held offshore and would bear a 32.8% repatriation tax, so it cannot fund the purchase price efficiently.

## 4. Key Operational Risks

1. **Product concentration (high):** iPhone was 66.3% of FY2015 net sales. The bear case cuts final-year EBITDA by $24.3B and IRR to -5.8%.
2. **Margin pressure (medium):** gross margin compression flows directly to cash available for debt service; in the bear case the final-year EBITDA margin is 1.5 percentage points lower than in the base case.
3. **Trapped cash and tax (high):** $186.9B of offshore cash stays trapped at close in the base case. The bull case, which repatriates offshore cash at close, reaches 21.3% IRR.
4. **Exit multiple (high):** the hurdle requires an exit at 14.1x EBITDA, versus entry at 8.0x.

## 5. Downside Sensitivities

Across the entry-multiple and leverage grid, the best outcome is 16.1% IRR. At entry multiples of 9.0x and above, additional leverage reduces returns: the cost of acquisition debt exceeds the unlevered return on the purchase price. The maximum entry multiple that clears the hurdle is 4.4x.

## 7. Final Recommendation

**Recommendation: PASS**

The transaction does not meet 2 of 3 investment criteria: base-case IRR 7.5% vs. 20% hurdle; base-case MOIC 1.43x vs. 2.0x minimum. Holding other assumptions at the base case, the hurdle would require entry at or below 4.4x, or an exit at 14.1x or higher, or the bull-case conditions (21.3% IRR).
